# Rigid body physics (gm-physics)

Status: Jolt implementation (`gm-physics`) with an IVP style impact listener, 1 of 5 `phys_*` harnesses within tolerance (`phys_drop_crate`; `phys_slide_crate` is 10 u short, `phys_drop_tilted`, `phys_slide_barrel`, `phys_toss_can` diverge after contact). Traces recorded on 2026.09.17.

Oracle: `prop_physics` spawned by the probe on a dedicated server, `PhysObj` transform, velocity and angular velocity recorded every server tick, plus mass, inertia, mass centre, material, volume, surface area and damping from the physics object.

## Measured on the real game

- Free fall is exactly `sv_gravity` 600 u/s^2 per tick: velocity -9, -18, -27 u/s after ticks 1, 2, 3; the position integrates with the new velocity (semi-implicit Euler: -0.135, -0.405, -0.81 u after ticks 1, 2, 3). Same integration as the player.
- `models/props_junk/wood_crate001a.mdl`: mass 30, material `wood_crate`, one convex, inertia about (3.59, 3.61, 3.62), mass centre offset about (-0.04, 0.006, -0.13), volume 62977, surface area 9497, linear and angular damping 0 reported by the object. Collision AABB about 40 x 40 x 40 (-20.03..19.94).
- A crate dropped flat from 84 u above the floor hits on tick 36 at -300 u/s and rebounds with +46 u/s (effective restitution about 0.15) while picking up a small spin from the off centre mass; it settles 0.4 u above the ideal resting height (-123.6 for a floor at -144 and a 20 u half extent: VPhysics keeps a collision margin) and goes to sleep after about 120 ticks.

- Air drag: while falling the crate gains 9.0, 8.99, 8.9 ... 7.8 u/s per tick, a loss that grows with the square of the speed: `v -= k |v| v dt` applied to the velocity gravity gives this tick, before the position integrates. `k = A(dir) / m` with `A(dir) = sum_i A_i |dir_i|` the collision box's projected area along the velocity in body axes and `A_i = face_i * dragAxisAreas_i` (the PHY solid header's per axis ratio of hull to box area, 1 for a box, about 0.77 across a cylinder, stored in IVP axis order). Fitted `k` per tick from the traces: crate flat 8.68e-4 per unit, crate tilted (20, 35, 10) 1.24e-3, can 5.25e-4, oil drum 5.5e-4 rising as it tips; gm-physics reproduces all four to the last digit, free flight positions agree within 1e-4 u over 33 ticks.
- Elasticity combines as the average of the two surfaces: wood (0.1, the crate's `Wood_Crate` inherits `Wood`) on concrete (0.2) gives the measured 0.15 rebound (46 u/s from 300). Jolt combines restitution as the max, so the world body carries 0 and each prop carries the average of its own and the world's elasticity.
- Resting bodies sit about 0.4 u (IVP's 1 cm tolerance) above the surface: world brush planes are pushed out by `REST_GAP` = 0.3937 u; ours rests at -123.66, the game at -123.60.
- A flat landing still picks up a lateral kick of 8.7 u/s in x and y in the game (IVP resolves the four corner contacts one after another), which drifts the crate 1.5 u before it sleeps; Jolt lands it dead flat. The rest tolerance is 2 u for that reason.
- Sliding friction: a crate pushed at 300 u/s along the concrete floor loses 6.0 u/s per tick (`slide_crate`), 0.64 g plus drag, i.e. the product of the two frictions (0.8 * 0.8), not Jolt's geometric mean; each body carries its friction squared so the pair comes out as the product. Ours loses 6.3 per tick and stops 104 u out against 112.5; the game's crate also hops once on the first tick and then yaws to 29 degrees while sliding straight in Jolt.
- Contact happens in the tick the hull crosses the surface, with the body left at the contact depth (the tilted crate stops at -115.77 where its corner reaches the floor). Jolt only predicts contacts 2 cm ahead and would land every prop a tick late and 2 to 3 u deep; `SPECULATIVE_CONTACT_M` is raised to 0.15 m through a small C++ shim (JoltC 0.3.1 does not expose `PhysicsSettings`), after which the contact tick matches in all four traces. PHY hulls get a 5 mm convex radius instead of Jolt's 5 cm so corners stay sharp.
- Open: the response after contact. An edge landing (`drop_tilted`) gets its spin mostly about x in the game (-242, -186, -56 deg/s) and about y in Jolt (-87, -231, 0), and comes to rest 10 u away with 23 degrees of yaw difference; the oil drum hitting its bottom edge is kicked to -x with a -206 deg/s yaw spin in the game and to +x with +120 in Jolt (which first vertex of the 24-gon takes the hit); the thrown can bounces differently. These are the solver's contact ordering and friction model, not geometry or free flight; they are kept as failing harnesses with their numbers rather than tuned by hand.

## Constraints (weld, rope)

Harness group `constraint_*` (3 traces on 2026.09.17: `weld_pair` two crates welded side by side and dropped, `rope_hang` a crate on an 80 u rope under a frozen crate, `stack_pair` a crate dropped on a resting crate). The probe spawns several props, applies `constraint.Weld` / `constraint.Rope` (the game creates `phys_constraint` / `phys_lengthconstraint`) and records every prop per tick.

- gm-physics: JoltC 0.3.1 has no constraint bindings, so the shim creates Jolt `FixedConstraint` (weld, auto detected point) and `DistanceConstraint` (rope, world space points, min 0 or the length when rigid) on the raw `PhysicsSystem`; `World::weld`, `World::rope`, `World::remove_constraint`.
- Measured and matched: the welded pair falls as one body with the same air drag per crate (positions equal to the tick until the landing); the roped crate swings with the same period for the first 40 ticks (within 1 u) and keeps the rope taut at 80 u.
- Rope (`constraint_rope_hang`, 80 u between the attachment points, slack at the start): the game's rope starts pulling at 79.59 u (tick 14, the crate goes from a free -125 u/s to 0, -28, -101), overshoots to 80.19 u at ticks 21 to 22 and settles at 80.03 by tick 40; our DistanceConstraint catches it a tick later at 80.79 u and settles at 80.05. Both swings reach the same far point within 1 u; the game keeps slightly more energy (second peak -462.1 against -463.7 in y). Jolt's limit spring (20 to 80 Hz, damping 0.3 to 1) reproduces neither the early pull nor the small overshoot and makes the swing worse (3.7 to 5.6 u at tick 300 against 3.0 u without), so the constraint stays hard; the measured curve points at IVP's constraint acting on the predicted next position, open.
- Stack (crate dropped from 60 u onto a resting crate, re-recorded on the flat floor at 823, -500 since the first spot sits over a brush where our static world is 4 u lower than the game's, see the open item below): both crates within 1 u and 0.3 degrees with the impact listener. Measured on the welded pair: the impact acts on the hit body alone within its tick (tick 31: the lower crate goes from -260 to -8, -8, +40 u/s while the welded upper crate keeps falling at -268 u/s), and the weld pulls the partner only from the next tick (-206 u/s at tick 32); Jolt's FixedConstraint reacts inside the same step, so our upper crate is already at -205 u/s at tick 31 and the pair ends 7 u further along +y and 3 degrees of yaw apart. Open: that one tick of constraint lag (the welded pair's landing, 7 u) and the rope swing after half a period (4.6 u, pendulum damping). 1 of 3 within tolerance.
- Displacements: the game's trace stops on the displacement surface, not on the brush under it (`util.TraceLine` with `MASK_SOLID_BRUSHONLY` at 600, -500 hits worldspawn at z = -148.00 while the brush below tops out at -152 and carries contents 0x100). Props get the displacement triangles as a static Jolt mesh (`brush::displacement_mesh`, wound along the face plane, pushed out by the rest gap). With it the stack dropped on that ground (`constraint_stack_disp`) matches the contact ticks (8 and 20) and the rest height (-127.67 against -127.60); the bottom crate slides 1.3 u the other way under the impact, 2.0 and 2.4 u against a 2 u tolerance. Player movement traces them too (`gm_collision` sweeps the box against each displacement triangle as a one-sided bevelled convex: triangle plane, bounds planes, edge-by-axis bevels, all pushed out by the extents); `move_walk_disp` walks from the displacement ground at 600, -500 (z = -147.969) up the 4 u step onto the brush at 823 and matches the server bit for bit.

## Impact response (IVP style, clean room)

The real game resolves the first touch of a pair as an *impact* at the contact points, not through the resting contact solver. Behaviour measured on the traces and confirmed by the symbol inventory of `vphysics_srv.so` (`IVP_Impact_Solver`, `IVP_Impact_System`, `IVP_Material_Manager`), implemented in the shim as a Jolt `ContactListener` (`gm-physics/src/shim.cpp`, toggle `World::set_impact_enabled`, runner `GMP_IMPACT=0` for A/B):

- Pair materials: elasticity is the product `e1 * e2`, friction the product `f1 * f2` (both clamped to [0, 1]). The separation speed after the impact is `sqrt(e1 e2) * |vn|`, which is where the measured 0.15 for wood on concrete came from (sqrt(0.1 * 0.2) = 0.141 plus the residual of the push loop).
- Impact set: every manifold point that reaches the surface within the step (a flat face lands on all four corners at once, an edge on one point). Speculative points further away than `|vn| dt` are left to Jolt.
- Push: estimate `-2 vn m_eff` from the virtual masses along the direction, applied in tenths round robin over the points until no point approaches any more (max 100 rounds), then one scaled push per point up to the wanted separation speed. Jolt's own restitution for that contact is zeroed; its solver only keeps the bodies apart afterwards.
- Friction tilt: `tan(angle) = f (1 + sqrt(e))`, push = `cos * n - sin * (tangential part of the unit relative velocity)` unless the motion is already inside that cone (then along the normal). Not renormalised.
- Static bodies get a virtual mass of 1e5 times the other body.
- Persisted contacts count as an impact only above 0.3 m/s approach speed; resting contact stays with Jolt. Repeated impact counting (IVP relaxes the elasticity towards 1 with the impact count) is left out: with Jolt resting contacts it made settling bodies bounce more every tick.

Measured with the probe's `PhysicsCollide` callback (every impact the engine resolves, with its point, normal and the velocity before it; now part of the `phys_*` traces): a flat crate dropped from 20 u impacts twice in one tick, first on one corner (speed 307.9 u/s, the velocity at the sub tick contact time), then 0.5 ms later on the opposite corner (velocity by then -31, +31, -216 u/s), and ends at -8.7, +8.7, +45.9 u/s; the upright barrel sliding at 285 u/s impacts first on the rim vertex at +x (perpendicular to the motion), which is where its yaw spin and sideways kick come from. So the game resolves one closest feature pair at a time and re-tests the others with the new velocities. The listener does the same with the real PHY hull vertices of the dynamic body against the contact plane: the vertex with the least time to reach the surface (`d / |vn|`, ties keep hull order) is resolved fully, then the rest are re-scanned, up to eight rounds. The first corner impact of the flat crate reproduces the game's state after it within 3 % (-22, +22, -223 against -31, +31, -216 u/s).

Body inertia now comes from the PHY header (`rotation_inertia` is per unit mass; the game's `GetInertia` is that times the mass: 3.6 kg m^2 for the crate against the 5.1 Jolt derives from a solid hull), set through the shim. `GetAngleVelocity` in the game is in body axes, the runner now writes ours the same way; the tilted crate's angular velocity after its first impact is then -260, -200, -60 against the game's -242, -186, -56 deg/s.

The floor under both corners is one world convex (checked with `GetMeshConvexes()` on the world, convex 1987 of 2095), so the second impact is the same pair; its larger impulse is the game's impact system re-pushing the pair's existing contact point (the first corner) along the normal while the new corner is resolved, with the separation speed measured at the centre. The listener keeps those contact points per pair (dropped when the pair separates) and does the same: the flat crate then leaves the floor at -17, +17, +34 u/s with 15 deg/s of spin against the game's -9, +9, +46 and 11.

Tie break between vertices at the same depth and speed: the game took the +x bottom corner of the flat crate and the +x rim vertex of the sliding barrel (the one perpendicular to its motion), so the listener picks the largest world x; measured on those two cases only, the real IVP rule (how the mindist picks its first synapse on a flat face) is still unknown. With it the barrel's first impact comes out at -66, 173, 87 u/s and -364, 0, -204 deg/s against the game's -60, 159, 105 and -394, -11, -206, and its trace error drops from 196 u to 44 u.

Props integrate rotation with the gyroscopic term (`ApplyGyroscopicForce`): the game's barrel, inertia 3.41 / 3.41 / 1.31, precesses in flight (body axes angular velocity -394, -11, -206 to -336, -146, -190 deg/s over 12 ticks with no contact), ours now follows it to within 6 deg/s; the can's rest orientation error drops from 179 to 46 degrees.

Effect: flat crate within tolerance (rest 1.2 u), tilted crate first impact right but the second one (3 ticks later) and the tumble after it differ (rest 4.3 u, 4.3 degrees). Open: after the second corner impact the game's crate leaves the floor at +45.9 u/s while ours still falls at -10; the game's impulse per impact is 2 to 8 times what the point's effective mass gives, so IVP's push (`test_push_core`, `calc_correct_virt_mass`, `get_relative_speed_vector`) is not the plain point impulse yet; the barrel and the can inherit the same gap.

## Solver settings do not close the gap (measured)

`World::set_solver` (shim) exposes Jolt's Baumgarte factor, velocity and position iterations and the restitution velocity threshold; the runner takes `GMP_SOLVER=baumgarte,vel_steps,pos_steps,min_restitution_velocity` for experiments. Sweeping Baumgarte 0.02 to 0.2, position steps 0 to 2, velocity steps 4 to 10 and the restitution threshold 0.1 to 1 m/s over all `phys_*`, `physgun_*` and `constraint_*` traces moves the errors by under 1 u (tilted crate stays at 5 to 6 u and 12 to 18 degrees, barrel at 135 to 200 u, can at 19 to 51 u, stack at 4 u). The mismatch is the contact model itself (IVP's impact and friction solver), not the amount of position correction, so the defaults stay and no tuning is baked in.

## Implementation notes

- Jolt 5.0 through `joltc-sys`/`rolt` 0.3.1, metres and Y up inside, Source units and Z up at the API: `jolt = (x, z, -y) * 0.0254`, the same mapping IVP used so PHY data converts one to one.
- World: every solid brush of model 0 (`SOLID`, `WINDOW`, `GRATE` contents) as a Jolt convex hull in one static compound body. Props: PHY ledges as convex hulls (compound when more than one), density from the PHY text section's `mass` and `volume`, friction and elasticity from `scripts/surfaceproperties.txt` with `base` inheritance.
- Gravity goes through the per body gravity factor (`600 * 0.0254 / 9.81`), one Jolt collision step per 0.015 s server tick.

## Target

Jolt (through `rolt` / `joltc-sys`, Jolt 5.0) with the world built from BSP brushes and props from their PHY convex hulls, surface properties from `scripts/surfaceproperties*.txt` (friction, elasticity, density), tuned until the `phys_*` traces match within tolerance. Bit exactness is not a goal here (different solver); the bar is the same contact tick, the same resting pose within 0.5 u, the same rebound within 10 percent, the same sleep behaviour.

## Impact time inside the step

The game integrates a body up to the moment its vertex reaches the surface with the old velocity and the rest of the tick with the new one: the tilted crate falls at -279.4 u/s, hits with its corner 1.75 u (0.0444 m) above the floor at the start of tick 34 and ends the tick at z -115.77 with vz -65, which is `-113.34 - 279.4 t - 65 (0.015 - t)` for `t = 6.8 ms`; its x (0 before, -108.9 after) gives the same `t`, and so does the barrel's z. Jolt solves the speculative contact first and then moves the body for the whole step with the new velocity (ours ended at -114.43, 1.3 u high, and the second corner then touched one tick late). The listener now records, per impacted body, `(v_old - v_new) t` and `(w_old - w_new) t` with `t = d / |vn|` of the first vertex it resolved (clamped to the step) and applies them after `update` (`gmp_apply_impact_offsets`): the crate's pose at tick 34 is then 699.1, -499.7, -115.7 and 18.3 / 34.2 / 7.4 degrees against the game's 699.1, -499.6, -115.8 and 18.5 / 34.3 / 7.7, and the second impact lands on tick 37 as in the game. The barrel does not follow the rule in y (it moved 2.0 u in its impact tick where either integration gives at least 2.4), left open.

Effect on the harnesses (before -> after): `phys_toss_can` 59.7 -> 51.9 u, `phys_slide_crate` 10.6 -> 9.2 u, `constraint_stack_disp` 3.8 -> 2.1 u, `phys_drop_crate` 1.19 (unchanged), `constraint_stack_pair` 0.65 / 0.99 -> 0.72 / 1.10 u; `phys_drop_tilted` rest 4.3 -> 7.2 u but rest angle 3.7 -> 1.3 degrees and the contact ticks now match; `phys_slide_barrel` 80 -> 101 u (its first impulse is already off: vz 87 against 105 u/s, w -364 against -394 deg/s). The impulses themselves are the remaining gap: scaling the PHY inertia 1.12 or 1.25 moves the first-impact velocities away from the game (tilted vx -101 -> -98 against -109), so the game's extra tangential velocity change is not an inertia matter. `GMP_NO_IMPACT_OFFSET=1` disables the offsets for experiments.

## The corner impulse itself

Fitting the game's response to the tilted crate's first corner impact (tick 34, gravity of the tick applied before the impact as the listener sees it, `v0 = -7.286 m/s`): the game's linear change is `J = m dv = (-83.0, 169.0, -31.7) N s` (Jolt axes), and `I^-1 (r x J)` with the corner's `r` and the PHY inertia gives the game's angular change to 0.2 % with the same direction (`cos = 1.0000`), so the game applies one point impulse at the hull vertex, as the listener does. The point's normal velocity after the impulse is `1.026 m/s = sqrt(0.0198) |vn0|`, the same restitution at the point as ours (`sqrt(e) |vn0|`, `e = 0.1 * 0.2`). The difference was the tangential part: the game's `|Jt| / Jn = 0.525` against our `0.508` (`Jn` 169.0 against 163.1), the direction of `Jt` exactly the tangential direction of `-r` (the way the corner slides as the body starts to turn), inside the friction cone on both sides (27.7 and 26.9 degrees against `atan(f (1 + sqrt e)) = 36.2`). It is the push size: the listener pushes in steps of a fraction of the point's stopping impulse and re-aims each push along the tilted direction; replaying this rule offline against the fitted `J`, a fraction of 0.1 misses by 4.5 %, 0.03 to 0.075 by under 0.5 % (0.01: 1.7 %, 0.02: 1.4 %, 0.035: 0.2 %, 0.05: 0.4 %, 0.06: 0.1 %, 0.075: 0.04 %, 0.1: 4.5 %, 0.125: 2.0 %; the curve is jagged because the iteration is discrete). The default is now 0.075 (`GMP_PUSH_FRACTION` overrides). The can's first impact (tick 73) is not a single vertex impulse (angular change 0.78 of a point impulse's, `cos 0.65`): it lands on its rim with an edge, not a vertex, left open.

Harnesses with the offsets and the new fraction (0.1 -> 0.075): `phys_drop_crate` 1.19 -> 1.12 u, `phys_drop_tilted` rest 7.2 -> 6.6 u (angle 3.0 degrees), `phys_slide_barrel` 101 -> 92 u, `phys_toss_can` 51.9 -> 57.6 u, `phys_slide_crate` 9.2 -> 10.7 u, `constraint_weld_pair` 7.2 -> 7.8 u, `constraint_stack_disp` 1.84 / 2.10 -> 1.79 / 2.12 u, `constraint_stack_pair` 0.72 / 1.10 u, physgun unchanged. Fractions 0.035 and 0.05 move these by 1 to 4 u either way (0.035 brings the welded pair to 2.8 / 3.1 u but the flat crate to 2.3 u, out of tolerance), so the downstream numbers do not discriminate within the measured range: what follows the first impact is governed by the second impact and edge contacts, still open.

## Angular drag

Recorded with ten `phys_spin_*` scenarios: can, crate and oil drum spun in free fall (start 450 u up, no linear velocity) about one body axis at 200 and 400 deg/s, 120 ticks each. The angular speed about body axis i decays as `dw_i/dt = -k_i w_i |w_i|` (the rate doubles with the speed: barrel x 0.100 /s at 200 deg/s and 0.200 /s at 400, can x 0.0112 and 0.0224, crate x 0.233 and 0.465), the direction of the loss is `-w` (cos 1.000), and `k_i` is

    k_i = (1/16) / I_i * ( a_k T(d_i, d_j, d_k) + a_j T(d_i, d_k, d_j) ),   T(l, w, h) = w^2 l^3 / 3 + w^4 l / 2 + l w^2 h^2

with `d` the PHY hull box in metres, `a` the PHY `dragAxisAreas` (Source axis order; the term swept along axis j carries the area of axis k), `I` the body inertia in kg m^2 and `w` in rad/s. The seven measured axes (can x, z; crate x, z; drum x, y, z) give the constant 0.0624, 0.0625, 0.0625, 0.0625, 0.0625, 0.0625, 0.0625 (spread 0.1 %); every other weighting of the areas tried (none, own axis, mean, geometric mean, product) spreads 7 to 60 %. The drum's x and y differ (0.0287 and 0.0340 /rad) only through its `dragAxisAreas` (1.0, 0.765, 1.0). gm-physics applies this per tick before the step (`angular_drag_factors`); the ten recordings then agree in flight to the last printed digit (0.1 deg/s, 0.01 u) over 77 to 102 ticks until the landing.

The landings of spinning bodies are a new open item: the can spinning at 197 deg/s about x leaves its first floor contact at -435 deg/s in the game and at -2700 in ours, the drum at -1341 against -192, the crate at (-982, -336, 76) against (233, 0, 0) with our contact one tick later. The `phys_spin_*` harnesses are kept red for that. Knock-on on the older harnesses: `phys_slide_barrel` 92 -> 117 u (the drum's spin now decays like the game's but its contacts still differ), `phys_toss_can` 57.6 -> 55.5 u, `phys_drop_tilted` rest 6.6 -> 6.7 u, `constraint_rope_hang` 4.6 -> 5.9 u, the rest unchanged. Experiment knob: `GMP_DRAG_DEBUG=1` prints each body's mass, drag areas, inertia and hull box.
